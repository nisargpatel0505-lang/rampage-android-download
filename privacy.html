Google sign-in and security

Sign-in uses Android Credential Manager and Firebase Authentication. Authentication requests use HTTPS. RAMPAGE keeps the native sign-in session and tokens in memory, not in its save file or preferences, and does not log tokens or account details. Closing the app process requires another sign-in. Google and Android manage their own account sessions separately.

The developer can administer RAMPAGE accounts through Firebase. Account information is used for authentication and account management, not for advertising or selling personal information. Google processes account data under its own applicable terms and privacy information: Google Privacy Policy and Firebase privacy information.

Retention and deletion

Your Firebase account remains until deletion is requested and completed. Firebase states that logged authentication IP addresses are retained for a few weeks and that other authentication data is removed from live and backup systems within 180 days after account deletion is initiated. Firebase Authentication processes data in the United States. See Firebase’s retention and location information.

Use Account → Delete account in the app, or use the account-deletion page. Deletion removes the RAMPAGE sign-in identity. It does not delete your Google account or local campaign progress. Signing out is not account deletion.

To erase local progress and preferences, use Android Settings → Apps → RAMPAGE → Storage → Clear storage (wording varies by phone). This resets the game on that device. Device backups are managed separately by your Android/Google backup settings. The developer cannot erase local-only data remotely.

These support pages

GitHub Pages serves this website and may process connection information to deliver and protect it; see the GitHub privacy statement. These pages do not add advertising or analytics trackers. The account-deletion page loads Firebase’s authentication code from Google when it is opened. Choosing verification opens Google sign-in; RAMPAGE’s web session is kept in memory and is cleared when you sign out or close the page. Google’s authentication service may use its own cookies.

Verifying a Google account that has never used RAMPAGE can create a temporary Firebase identity. The deletion page requests your permission first and removes that newly created identity as part of the request. You can instead request deletion by email without using web sign-in.
